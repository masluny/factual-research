Vitamin D and acute respiratory infections

Overall effect

An individual participant data meta-analysis found that vitamin D supplementation reduced the risk of acute respiratory tract infection Martineau et al., 2017, p. 1. Across 10 933 participants, the adjusted odds ratio was 0.88 (95% CI 0.81 to 0.96) Martineau et al., 2017.

The most recent update, however, combined 40 placebo-controlled trials and found that vitamin D did not significantly affect overall ARI risk (OR 0.94, 95% CI 0.88 to 1.00) Jolliffe et al., 2025, p. 5. Large pragmatic trials were also null: a UK test-and-treat trial in 6200 people was not associated with protection against acute respiratory infection Jolliffe et al., 2022, and a trial of daily supplementation in older adults joined other recent null results Camargo et al., 2024.

Dosing

Earlier analyses suggested that daily or weekly regimens were protective (OR 0.81), whereas bolus doses were not (OR 0.97) Martineau et al., 2017, p. 1. The 2025 update did not confirm this effect modification Jolliffe et al., 2025, p. 5.

Evidence table

Bottom line

The benefit, if any, appears small and is now uncertain; the certainty of evidence for an overall protective effect is moderate at best.
