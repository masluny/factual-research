# Vitamin D and acute respiratory infections

## Overall effect

An individual participant data meta-analysis found that vitamin D supplementation reduced the risk of acute respiratory tract infection [1, p. 1]. Across 10 933 participants, the adjusted odds ratio was 0.88 (95% CI 0.81 to 0.96) [1].

The most recent update, however, combined 40 placebo-controlled trials and found that vitamin D did not significantly affect overall ARI risk (OR 0.94, 95% CI 0.88 to 1.00) [2, p. 5]. Large pragmatic trials were also null: a UK test-and-treat trial in 6200 people was not associated with protection against acute respiratory infection [3], and a trial of daily supplementation in older adults joined other recent null results [4].

## Dosing

Earlier analyses suggested that daily or weekly regimens were protective (OR 0.81), whereas bolus doses were not (OR 0.97) [1, p. 1]. The 2025 update did not confirm this effect modification [2, p. 5].

**Dosing regimen and protection against ARI**

| Source | Regimen | Effect | Interpretation |

## Bottom line

The benefit, if any, appears small and is now uncertain; the certainty of evidence for an overall protective effect is moderate at best.
